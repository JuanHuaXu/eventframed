# Claim Rescue and Replacement Program v1

## Status

This program responds to the falsified and inconclusive entries in the
EventFrame claim register. A replacement is not a retroactive validation of the
old proposition. Old failures remain in the evidence record. Every replacement
receives a new proposition, an implementation invariant, and a falsifier.

## R1: bounded frontier-all with selective deep work

The bounded nominated frontier is the unit of cheap Bayesian work. Every
evidence-ready member receives the constant-state update. Activation scores may
nominate expensive review, particle inference, model comparison, or graph
expansion, but may not suppress the cheap update.

Falsifier: under the same bounded frontier and evidence, the default policy
updates fewer evidence-ready members than frontier-all, or its quality is
inferior to frontier-all outside a predeclared non-inferiority margin.

## R2: posterior-conditioned post-retrieval correction

Bayesian inference does not claim to repair retrieval nomination. EventFrame
may change ordering only inside the bounded nominated frontier, after the
LibraVDB ranking contract, through versioned posterior, residual, and graph
rank deltas. Packing runs after those deltas.

Falsifier: a relevant nominated candidate has a favorable certified correction
but cannot move before packing, or a non-nominated candidate is silently
introduced by EventFrame.

## R3: residuals as abstaining online experts

Residuals are optional local experts. Eligibility requires support, component
motion, epoch, age, and both a time-uniform positive mean-gain bound and the
existing improvement-frequency bound. The applied correction is shrunk by the
certified gain strength. A changepoint invalidates the epoch. Failed gates run
in shadow mode and cannot affect law or rank.

The old criterion limiting the fraction of individually harmful applications
to five percent is retained as a diagnostic but is not generally achievable
under an unannounced reversal: the first post-change prediction necessarily
precedes the evidence announcing the change. The replacement criterion bounds
cumulative excess Brier loss against the baseline and requires positive
untouched mean gain.

Falsifier: the replacement has negative mean gain, exceeds its cumulative harm
budget, or applies after an invalidating epoch or motion change.

## R4: equivalence-bounded partial pooling

The group comparator uses evidence of practical equivalence inside a frozen
region of practical equivalence, not evidence for exact equality. Its output
includes a bounded borrowing weight. `split` forces zero borrowing;
`uncertain` permits only conservative partial pooling; `share` still cannot
merge posterior keys without an external Anti-Pigeon certificate.

Falsifier: ordinary compatible groups remain undecidable at declared support,
divergent groups receive material borrowing, or comparison mutates authority.

## R5: independent omitted-influence audit

The audit population is explicit. Each omitted or sentinel event has a
predeclared, nonzero Bernoulli inclusion probability independent of its measured
influence. Normalized Jensen-Shannon divergence compares local and expanded
laws. A Horvitz-Thompson mean and an anytime-valid upper bound certify only the
declared finite population and exact durable recall-journal query. Every runtime
population member must be absent from that journal's nominations. Zero-probability,
unknown-size, stale-journal, and cross-query populations are not certified.

Falsifier: coverage falls below the frozen level under the declared generator,
selection depends on observed influence, or never-nominated events have zero
support while the report claims corpus coverage.

## R6: graph-consumed predictive snapping

Published predictive graph edges produce a bounded neighbor-propagation
feature over already nominated candidates. The feature enters the activation
record and the post-contract rank delta. The graph never nominates an event
that the retrieval contract omitted. Publication and rollback remain atomic.

Contract 13 additionally types graph effects as symmetric `compatible`,
directional `supports`, or directional `supersedes`. Supersession can produce a
negative rank delta only for its target. It is predictive demotion, not causal
orientation, and remains subject to graph weighting, elastic modulation,
clipping, publication evidence, and rollback.

Falsifier: a graph change leaves all graph features and deltas invariant in a
fixture with nonzero connected scores, or rollback fails to restore the prior
ordering and version behavior.

## R7: priority as a deployment constraint

Priority is evaluated as a separate constraint, not hidden inside one scalar
score. A candidate policy passes only if the upper confidence bound on its
high-priority miss increase is within the frozen ceiling and its overall recall
loss is within tolerance. Priority is fixed before outcomes.

Falsifier: a policy is promoted from positive weighted utility while violating
either constraint.

## R8: structured frames as a matched multi-view ablation

Canonical structured 5W1H replaces raw event text as the operational retrieval
corpus in contract version 12; raw text remains opaque metadata and final
payload. The proposed ablation still holds source content, model, embedding
budget, candidate universe, and ranking contract fixed across raw-text,
structured, combined, and shuffled-field variants. Interpretability requires
independent blinded ratings.

Falsifier: the combined representation fails its frozen predictive criterion or
independent interpretability criterion. Fewer than three independent
trajectories remains inconclusive, not favorable evidence.

## Execution order

1. Preserve frontier-all and post-retrieval correction as reference behavior.
2. Add residual abstention and cumulative-harm reporting.
3. Add equivalence-bounded partial pooling without changing authority.
4. Add the independent omitted-influence estimator.
5. Feed bounded graph propagation into activation and rank deltas.
6. Add priority-constraint and structured-ablation evaluation contracts.
7. Run unit, integration, race, benchmark, design-seed, and untouched
   confirmation-seed tests. Report each replacement separately from the old
   failed proposition.
